\chapter{Conclusion}\label{ch:conclusions}

This dissertation developed a differentiable forward solver for
voltammetric simulation and integrated it with gradient-based Bayesian
inference to solve the electrochemical inverse problem. The work made
three contributions. First, a custom adjoint rule for the banded linear
solve yields exact gradients at $O(n)$ cost per timestep with minimal
memory overhead, avoiding the $O(mn)$ storage of na\"ive taping
(\cref{ch:adjoint}). Second, coupling this solver with NUTS produces
full posterior distributions over the physical parameters, quantifying
uncertainty, revealing correlations, and detecting non-identifiability
--- information that point estimates cannot provide
(\cref{ch:bayesian,ch:hmc}). Third, the framework was applied to three
reaction mechanisms of increasing complexity: a single electron transfer
(3~parameters), a heterogeneous reaction (7~parameters), and an
adsorption reaction (9~parameters). Under equal runtime budgets,
the NUTS/RWMH effective-sample-size ratio grew from
$1.5$--$2.4\times$ in three dimensions (\cref{tab:electron-ess}) to
$\sim\!3\times$ in seven (\cref{tab:het-ess}) and a median of
$2.7\times$ in nine (\cref{tab:ads-ess}). Each forward solver was
verified against analytical solutions before inference: the
Randles--\v{S}ev\v{c}\'{\i}k peak current for electron transfer, the
analytical voltammogram of \cref{ch:heterogeneous} for the heterogeneous
case, and the monolayer limit for adsorption.

The adsorption reaction also illustrated how the framework adapts to
numerical constraints. The Langmuir coupling terms make the discrete
system nonlinear, preventing a direct banded solve. A backward implicit
linearisation provided an AD-compatible approximation two to three
orders of magnitude more accurate than an explicit alternative, at a
modest 33\% runtime overhead. Inference with this approximate solver
introduced no detectable bias relative to the Newton reference at the
noise levels considered.

\section{Further Work}\label{sec:further-work}

\paragraph{Experimental data.}
All results in this dissertation use synthetic data generated from the
forward model. Real voltammograms introduce model misspecification:
capacitive charging current, ohmic drop through the solution resistance,
and electrode surface roughness are absent from the simulations. Applying
the framework to experimental data would test the robustness of the
posterior distributions under model--data mismatch, and would likely
require extending the forward model or introducing nuisance parameters.

\paragraph{Alternative kinetic models.}
The Butler--Volmer equation was used throughout as the constitutive
kinetic law. Marcus--Hush--Chidsey
theory~\cite{compton2014understanding} provides a more
physically grounded model at the cost of a numerical integral in the
rate expression. Bayesian model comparison between Butler--Volmer and
Marcus--Hush--Chidsey --- using, for example, marginal likelihood
ratios --- would allow the data to discriminate between kinetic
models. Chen et~al.~\cite{chen2026differentiable} have begun this
programme with gradient-based optimisation; extending it to full
posterior inference is a natural next step.

\paragraph{Neural operator surrogates.}
The forward solver computes the full concentration field $C(X,T)$, but
the likelihood depends only on the surface flux $J(T)$. A neural
operator (e.g.\ DeepONet or Fourier neural operator) trained on solver
output could emulate the parameter-to-current map at a fraction of the
cost. Such surrogates are differentiable by construction and
NUTS-compatible, and could reduce the per-sample cost by orders of
magnitude, making inference over larger parameter spaces or more complex
reaction networks practical.

\paragraph{Alternative samplers.}
NUTS is not the only gradient-based sampler. Microcanonical Langevin
Monte Carlo (MCLMC) and other adaptive HMC variants may offer further
efficiency gains, particularly in the high-dimensional,
correlated posteriors encountered in the adsorption case, where the
NUTS advantage over RWMH was reduced for the
adsorption/desorption rate constants.

\medskip

The framework developed here is not specific to the three reactions
studied. Any voltammetric forward model that can be expressed as a
sequence of banded linear solves admits the same adjoint treatment, and
the inference workflow applies without modification. As differentiable
programming becomes standard infrastructure in the computational
sciences~\cite{kitchin2025beyond}, coupling differentiable PDE solvers
with gradient-based samplers offers a systematic route to full
uncertainty quantification for electrochemical inverse problems.
