\chapter{Heterogeneous Reaction}\label{ch:heterogeneous}

This chapter applies the inference framework to a reaction involving two
electron transfers coupled by an irreversible heterogeneous chemical
step. The parameter space grows from three to seven dimensions, providing
a first test of how the relative efficiency of NUTS and RWMH scales with
dimension. Because the methodology has been established, we focus on
what is new: the reaction scheme, the coupled PDE system, the reordering
that yields a pentadiagonal linear system, and the inference results. A
more detailed treatment of surface processes -- including explicit
surface concentrations and adsorption/desorption kinetics -- is deferred
to \cref{ch:adsorption}.

\section{Reaction Scheme}\label{sec:het-scheme}

We consider the $\mathrm{EC}_{\mathrm{irr,het}}\mathrm{E}$
mechanism~\cite{compton2014understanding}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:het-scheme}
  \begin{gathered}
    \mathrm{A} + \mathrm{e}^{-} \rightleftharpoons \mathrm{B}, \\
    \mathrm{B} \xrightarrow{k_{\mathrm{het}}} \mathrm{C}, \\
    \mathrm{C} + \mathrm{e}^{-} \rightleftharpoons \mathrm{D}.
  \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Species~$\mathrm{A}$ is reduced to~$\mathrm{B}$; $\mathrm{B}$ is
converted irreversibly to~$\mathrm{C}$ by a surface-catalysed process
with rate constant $k_{\mathrm{het}}$~($\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$); and
$\mathrm{C}$ undergoes a second electron transfer to form~$\mathrm{D}$.
The chemical step occurs only at the electrode surface, so bulk transport
of all four species is governed by Fick's second law. This mechanism
arises, for example, in the electroreduction of halonitroaromatic
compounds in aprotic media~\cite{compton2014understanding}.

  $\mathrm{EC}_{\mathrm{irr,het}}\mathrm{E}$ reaction]{Schematic of

Each electron transfer has its own $\alpha^{(i)}$, $K_0^{(i)}$, and
$\theta_f^{(i)}$. Together with the dimensionless heterogeneous rate
constant $K_{\mathrm{het}}$, the parameter vector is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:het-params}
  \phi = \bigl(\alpha^{(1)},\, K_0^{(1)},\, \theta_f^{(1)},\,
    \alpha^{(2)},\, K_0^{(2)},\, \theta_f^{(2)},\,
  K_{\mathrm{het}}\bigr) \in \mathbb{R}^7.
\end{equation}

\section{Mathematical Description}\label{sec:het-maths}

Under the equal-diffusion assumption ($d_j = 1$), each species satisfies
the dimensionless diffusion equation~\cref{eq:nd_diffusion}. Unlike the
single-electron case, the heterogeneous step breaks stoichiometric
conservation at the surface -- $\mathrm{B}$ is consumed both by
oxidation to~$\mathrm{A}$ and by conversion to~$\mathrm{C}$ -- so the
system cannot be reduced to a single PDE and all four equations must be
solved.

Initially only $\mathrm{A}$ is present at bulk concentration:
$C_\mathrm{A}(0,X) = 1$, $C_\mathrm{B} = C_\mathrm{C} = C_\mathrm{D}
= 0$. These values are also imposed at $X = X_{\max}$.

\subsection{Electrode Boundary Conditions}

We apply the scalings of \cref{tab:normalisation}, defining
$K_{\mathrm{red}}^{(i)}$ and $K_{\mathrm{ox}}^{(i)}$ for each electron
transfer as in~\cref{eq:kred_kox}, and the dimensionless heterogeneous
rate constant as $K_{\mathrm{het}} = k_{\mathrm{het}}\, r_e /
D_\mathrm{A}$. The boundary conditions at $X = 0$
are~\cite{compton2014understanding}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:het-bc-nondim}
  \begin{aligned}
    \left(\frac{\partial C_\mathrm{A}}{\partial X}\right)_{X=0}
    &= K_{\mathrm{red}}^{(1)}\, C_\mathrm{A}
    - K_{\mathrm{ox}}^{(1)}\, C_\mathrm{B}, \\[4pt]
    \left(\frac{\partial C_\mathrm{B}}{\partial X}\right)_{X=0}
    &= -K_{\mathrm{red}}^{(1)}\, C_\mathrm{A}
    + \bigl(K_{\mathrm{ox}}^{(1)} + K_{\mathrm{het}}\bigr)\,
    C_\mathrm{B}, \\[4pt]
    \left(\frac{\partial C_\mathrm{C}}{\partial X}\right)_{X=0}
    &= K_{\mathrm{red}}^{(2)}\, C_\mathrm{C}
    - K_{\mathrm{ox}}^{(2)}\, C_\mathrm{D}
    - K_{\mathrm{het}}\, C_\mathrm{B}, \\[4pt]
    \left(\frac{\partial C_\mathrm{D}}{\partial X}\right)_{X=0}
    &= -K_{\mathrm{red}}^{(2)}\, C_\mathrm{C}
    + K_{\mathrm{ox}}^{(2)}\, C_\mathrm{D},
  \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where all surface concentrations are evaluated at $(T, 0)$. The
$K_{\mathrm{het}}$ term appears only in the $\mathrm{B}$ and $\mathrm{C}$
equations and is the sole coupling between the two electron-transfer
subsystems.

\subsection{Current}

The measured current includes contributions from both electrochemical
steps and the heterogeneous conversion:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:het-current}
  J = -\left\{
    \left(\frac{\partial C_\mathrm{A}}{\partial X}\right)_{X=0}
    + \left(\frac{\partial C_\mathrm{C}}{\partial X}\right)_{X=0}
    + K_{\mathrm{het}}\, C_\mathrm{B}(T, 0)
  \right\}.
\end{equation}
The $K_{\mathrm{het}}\, C_\mathrm{B}$ term accounts for the electron
transferred indirectly when $\mathrm{B}$ is converted to~$\mathrm{C}$ at
the surface.

\section{Pentadiagonal System}\label{sec:het-disc}

Each species is discretised on the same exponentially expanding mesh of
\cref{sec:discretisation}. The four species are coupled only through the
boundary conditions at $X = 0$; in the interior each satisfies an
independent tridiagonal system. A natural species-by-species ordering of
the unknowns places the $K_{\mathrm{het}}$ coupling entries $n$ positions
from the main diagonal, far outside any narrow band.

The bandwidth is reduced by interleaving the unknowns: $\mathrm{A}$ and
$\mathrm{B}$ are indexed from $X_{n-1}$ down to $X_0$ (far field to
electrode), while $\mathrm{C}$ and $\mathrm{D}$ run from $X_0$ to
$X_{n-1}$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:het-ordering}
  \mathbf{C} = \bigl(
    C_\mathrm{A}^{n-1}, C_\mathrm{B}^{n-1}, \ldots,
    C_\mathrm{A}^{0}, C_\mathrm{B}^{0},\,
    C_\mathrm{C}^{0}, C_\mathrm{D}^{0}, \ldots,
    C_\mathrm{C}^{n-1}, C_\mathrm{D}^{n-1}
  \bigr)^T.
\end{equation}
The electrode-surface nodes of all species are now adjacent, and the
$K_{\mathrm{het}}$ coupling between $C_\mathrm{B}^0$ and
$C_\mathrm{C}^0$ falls within two positions of the main diagonal. The
result is a pentadiagonal system solved in $O(n)$ per timestep. As noted
in \cref{sec:pentadiagonal}, its transpose is again pentadiagonal, so the
adjoint system is solved at the same cost.

\section{Inference Setup}\label{sec:het-inference}

The inference workflow follows \cref{sec:workflow}. The seven parameters
are transformed: $\alpha^{(i)} \mapsto \operatorname{logit}(\alpha^{(i)})$,
$K_0^{(i)} \mapsto \log K_0^{(i)}$, $K_{\mathrm{het}} \mapsto \log
K_{\mathrm{het}}$, with $\theta_f^{(i)}$ untransformed. Synthetic data
are generated at $\phi^* = (0.5,\, 15.0,\, 0.2,\, 0.5,\, 6.0,\, 0.4,\,
25.0)$ with $\sigma = 1000$ and $\eta = 0.02$. Under equal runtime
budgets this gives $2{,}800$ NUTS samples and $40{,}000$ RWMH samples.

\section{Results}\label{sec:het-results}

\subsection{Solver Verification}\label{sec:het-verification}

We verify the solver in two limiting
cases~\cite{prieto1997electrode}. When $K_{\mathrm{het}} \to 0$ the
chemical step is inactive and the system reduces to a single electron
transfer, whose peak current and potential match
\cref{eq:irreversible-peak}. When $K_{\mathrm{het}} \to \infty$ with
$K_0^{(2)} \to \infty$, every molecule of $\mathrm{B}$ is instantly
converted to $\mathrm{C}$ and reduced to $\mathrm{D}$, giving a
two-electron transfer with twice the peak current.
\Cref{fig:het-analytical} confirms both limits.

\subsection{Posterior Distributions}\label{sec:het-posteriors}

\Cref{fig:het-corner} shows selected pairwise marginal posteriors.
Both samplers recover the true values across all seven dimensions;
the diagonal overlays both marginals, while the
off-diagonal shows the NUTS joint structure alone for clarity.
Within each electron transfer, $\alpha^{(i)}$ and $K_0^{(i)}$ are
negatively correlated, consistent with the compensation effect of
\cref{sec:bv-effects}. Between electron transfers, the rate constants
$K_0^{(1)}$ and $K_0^{(2)}$ also correlate negatively: an increase in
the rate of the first electron transfer can be offset by a decrease in
the second, since both contribute additively to the measured current
in~\cref{eq:het-current}.

The $K_{\mathrm{het}}$ marginal has a width comparable to that of
$K_0^{(2)}$ (approximately $0.5$ units), despite its larger value
($K_{\mathrm{het}} = 25$ vs $K_0^{(2)} = 6$). By contrast, $K_0^{(1)}$
has a width of approximately $5$ units around its true value of $15$.
The heterogeneous rate constant thus remains identifiable even at large
values, reflecting the distinct signature of the chemical step in the
current.

\subsection{Sampling Efficiency}\label{sec:het-ess}

\Cref{fig:het-ess} compares ESS growth for NUTS and RWMH on a normalised
cost axis. \Cref{tab:het-ess} reports the final values: NUTS achieves
$2.7$--$3.6\times$ the ESS of RWMH across all seven parameters,
up from $1.5$--$2.4\times$ in the three-parameter case. The widening
gap is consistent with the scaling discussed in
\cref{sec:hmc-results}: in seven dimensions the RWMH proposal must
shrink further to maintain its acceptance rate, while NUTS continues
to take gradient-informed steps of comparable length.

\section{AC Voltammetry}\label{sec:het-ac}

In AC voltammetry a sinusoidal perturbation is superimposed on the DC
ramp:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ac-potential}
  \theta(T) = \theta_{\mathrm{DC}}(T) + \Delta\theta\,
  \sin(2\pi f\, T),
\end{equation}
where $\Delta\theta$ is the amplitude and $f$ the frequency. The
oscillatory component of the current probes the electrode kinetics on a
faster timescale than the DC sweep alone, providing additional
information about the rate constants and transfer
coefficients. Gavaghan et~al.~\cite{gavaghan2018bayesian} demonstrated
this for the single-electron reaction; we extend the comparison to the
heterogeneous case.

We use $\Delta\theta = 0.5$ and $128$ oscillations over
the experiment, with the temporal grid chosen to ensure at least $10$
points per oscillation so that the sinusoidal waveform is adequately
resolved. No changes are required to the forward solver or inference
workflow: the perturbation enters only through the time-dependent
$K_{\mathrm{red}}^{(i)}$ and $K_{\mathrm{ox}}^{(i)}$ in the boundary
conditions.

\Cref{fig:ac-voltam} shows the resulting voltammogram at the true
parameters; the oscillations are largest near the formal potentials,
where the Butler--Volmer kinetics are most sensitive to potential changes.

\Cref{fig:ac-dc-comparison} overlays the marginal posteriors from DC and
AC data using NUTS. The AC posteriors are narrower for all parameters,
with modes more closely aligned with the true values, consistent with
Gavaghan et~al.~\cite{gavaghan2018bayesian}. The oscillatory
perturbation probes the electrode kinetics at the frequency~$f$, a
timescale independent of the DC sweep rate~$\sigma$, providing
additional constraint on the rate constants and transfer coefficients.
The DC posteriors are adequate for describing the current, as the
results of \cref{sec:het-posteriors} confirm, but the AC data
tighten them further.
