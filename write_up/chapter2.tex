\section{Mass Transport}

For a macroband electrode:

\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial c_j}{\partial t} = D_j \frac{\partial^2 c_j}{\partial x^2}
  \label{eq:masstransport_macroband}
\end{equation}
Where $D_j$ is the diffusion coefficient associated with the species
$j$. In the case of \autoref{eq:eMechanism}, we obtain a mass transport
equation for each of species A and B:

$$
\begin{cases}
  \frac{\partial c_A}{\partial t} = D_A \frac{\partial^2 c_A}{\partial x^2} \\
  \frac{\partial c_B}{\partial t} = D_B \frac{\partial^2 c_B}{\partial x^2}
\end{cases}
$$
\section{Boundary Conditions}
Initially, we will assume we have only the species A in solution
which is equal to the bulk concentration, $c_A^*$ and that we have
none of species B.

\section{Equal Diffusion Coefficients}

Fick's first law states that the diffusive flux, $j_{x_1}$, through a
plane normal to the $x$-axis at the point $x_1$ is equal to the
product of the diffusion coefficient and the concerntration gradient
at that point.

\begin{equation}
  j_{x_1} = -D \left( \frac{\partial c}{\partial x} \right)_{x=x_1}
\end{equation}

Where the minus sign indicates the flux is down the local
concerntation gradient. We assume that initially the experiment
contains only species A at a uniform concerntration, the bulk
concerntration indicated by $c^*_A$. By conservation of mass, the
amount of A lost at the electrode surface equals the amount of B gained:

\begin{equation}
  \left( -D_A \frac{\partial c_A}{\partial x} \right)_{x=0}  = \left( D_B
  \frac{\partial c_B}{\partial x} \right)_{x=0}
\end{equation}

If we assume equal diffusion coefficients $D = D_A = D_B$. Then the
problem simplifies to:

\begin{equation}
  \left( - \frac{\partial c_A}{\partial x} \right)_{x=0}  = \left(
  \frac{\partial c_B}{\partial x} \right)_{x=0}
\end{equation}

Therefore it is necessarily true that: $c^*_A = c_A + c_B$. So we can
only model the concerntration of A and automatically recover the
concerntration of B. We will now be dropping the subscript of the
species and assume we are always referring to A.

\section{Initial Conditions}
As already mentioned, we are assuming that initially there is only A
present in the solution. Thus the initial conditions are:
\begin{equation}
  c(x, t = 0) = c^*
\end{equation}

\section{Boundary Conditions}
In the spacial dimension we use Einstein's work on Brownian motion.
$0 \geq x \geq 6 \sqrt{D t_{\text{max}}}$.

The first boundary condition we say that there is no change in the
concerntration when we are sufficiently far away from the surface of
the working electrode:

\begin{equation}
  c(x = x_{\text{max}}, t) = c^*
\end{equation}

At the surface electrode we have the Butler-Volmer equation:
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
    \left(-\frac{\partial c}{\partial x}\right)_{x=0}
    &= k_0\Bigg[
      c_A\exp\!\left((-\alpha\frac{F}{RT}\left(E_{\mathrm{eff}}(t)-E_0\right)\right)\\
        &\qquad-
        (1-c)\exp\!\left((1-\alpha)\frac{F}{RT}\left(E_{\mathrm{eff}}(t)-E_0\right)\right)
      \Bigg]
    \end{aligned}
  \end{equation}
